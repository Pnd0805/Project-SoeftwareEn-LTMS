## 2. Fixed on the frontend

### R10 · Assign referees from the draw page

There was no referee UI on the draw page at all. The only way to staff a match
was to open its Fixture page one at a time, which also meant no view of which
matches were still uncovered.

New `src/features/tournament/manage/MatchRefereePlanner.tsx`, mounted under the
Draw subtab. It lists every match in the tournament with its accepted referees,
its outstanding requests, and a picker per row.

**Later rounds can be staffed before anyone knows who plays in them.** That is
the part worth recording, because it was the half of the request we were least
sure the API supported. `assertMatchChangeable` in
`services/refereeRequest.service.ts` requires the match to be `scheduled` with a
future start and end — it says nothing about teams. So a slot with both places
empty gets the picker; a slot with no kick-off is told to set one first, since
that is the condition that actually blocks FR02.

A request is never drawn as an assignment: a row reads "Waiting for their
answer" until the referee accepts, which matters because FR02 only creates a
request and FR06 is what applies it.

Verified live on tournament 22: requested มานะ for match 14 → row read
"Waiting for their answer" → cancelled → back to "Available".

This also closes the older **R04**, which asked for the same thing. They share
the component; do not schedule them separately.

### R11 · Reject then verify again, and a reason field

Two separate causes.

1. A row whose status was `rejected` had **no action at all** for a referee, in
   both the roster panel and the roster-less console. A referee who rejected the
   wrong person had no way back, even though M19 has overwritten a rejected row
   since OD-19.
2. The Reject button posted a hardcoded `'Rejected by the referee'`. M15 has
   always required a reason, so the mandatory field was being filled with a
   constant instead of the referee's words.

Added `RevokeCheckinModal` (reason required, explains that the player can check
in again), gave a rejected row the M19 path with a banner stating that it
overwrites the row and clears the rejection, and put the referee's own decision
errors on screen — they were silent before, which is how a failing action looks
identical to a dead button.

Verified live on match 7: rejected a player with a typed reason → row went
Rejected → verified by hand → row came back Checked in with the note showing.

### R12 · Fixture page scope

The page already edited the schedule and the referees. The gate was wrong:
`open = m.checkedIn === 0` let a `checkin_open` match with nobody checked in yet
present an editable form that M06 then refused with 409
`MATCH_NOT_CHANGEABLE`, and the locked branch hid the referees entirely.

The gate is now `m.status === 'scheduled'`, which is M06's and FR02's actual
rule. The locked view states which status it is in and still lists the
officials.

Two things found while in there:

- `can.editFixture` conflated "not the organizer" with "too late to change", so
  the organizer of a finished match was told the fixture was not theirs to set.
  Split.
- The Request button was disabled once accepted + pending reached `needed`.
  BR-10's count is a minimum, not a cap, so an organizer wanting a spare
  official found a dead button with nothing explaining it. Removed; the real
  constraint is the time conflict, which the server checks.

### R13 · Double forfeit lifecycle

`ResultTrail` counted only `verified` as settled. A `walkover` row — which is
what M17 writes for both a one-sided walkover and a double forfeit — fell
through to "Waiting on the winning team's leader. Until then nothing moves." and
never ticked Bracket updated. The bracket had in fact already advanced: M17
writes the result and places teams in one transaction.

A forfeit now reads as settled without play, says no confirmation is needed
(it is the organizer's ruling, not a played result), and where there is no
winner says that nobody advances and the slot it fed stays empty.

Verified live: forfeited match 7 with both squads short of `min_members`; the
Progress tab reads correctly end to end.

### R15 · Confirming a result

**We could not reproduce a blocked confirmation.** Tried the winning team's
leader on an on-site match twice — match 12 as `playerA1@ku.th`, match 9 as
`p9225@ku.th`. Both times the Confirm button was present and enabled, the click
went through, and the bracket advanced. `POST /matches/:id/result/verify` from
the winner's leader was accepted every time; `requireCanVerifyResult` does
exactly what BR-13 says.

What was certainly broken: **neither Confirm nor Dispute had anywhere to show an
error.** A refused request left the screen completely unchanged, which from the
user's side is indistinguishable from a button that does nothing. Added
`SignOffError`, with named handling for:

- `SAME_PERSON_CANNOT_VERIFY` — the one an account that is both match referee
  and team leader hits every time, since it cannot verify what it submitted
- `WRONG_SUBMITTER_ROLE`
- `MATCH_RESULT_ALREADY_VERIFIED` — somebody signed off while the page was open

**One question for whoever filed R15:** was the match on-site or online? On an
online match the referee is the confirmer by BR-13 (`matches.mode = 'online'` →
the team leader submits, the referee verifies), so a winning leader seeing only
Dispute is the design, not a defect. If it was on-site we would like the match
id, because we could not make it fail.

### R16 · Dispute resolution and throw-out

Two causes, both confirmed in the browser before and after.

1. All three resolve buttons were disabled until the Why box had text, with no
   hint, no title and no asterisk. The panel simply looked dead. The requirement
   is now stated in the field label and in a banner, and the disabled buttons
   carry a title.
2. The real dead end. After a throw-out the backend puts the match in
   **`result_rejected`** — a value that was **missing from our `MatchStatusEnum`**
   although it has been in `schema.sql` all along. Three things followed from
   that: `matchStateOf` fell through to "Scheduled" (reading as though the match
   had never happened), `ActionPanel` had no branch for a `rejected` result, and
   `can.submitResult` required `checkin_open`/`in_progress` — so **nobody could
   record the match again**, although `requireCanSubmitResult` has no status
   check at all and was waiting for exactly that submission.

Added the status and its label, a panel that says what happened and why, and the
result form back for whoever may submit. Also stopped drawing the thrown-out
score on the scorebug, which was still announcing a score the organizer had just
cancelled.

Verified live twice, on two different database states: organizer throws the
result out → referee gets the form → enters a new score → winning leader
confirms.

### Note on the QA baseline

Partway through today's verification the database was rolled back to the QA
baseline, which discarded the live test writes from the first pass. Two things
came out of that worth knowing:

- The restored baseline has **127 `application_players` rows**. The state we
  started from had none, which is why M19 answered `403 NOT_IN_APPROVED_ROSTER`
  on every match we tried first. That was stale data, not a defect — anyone
  reproducing a check-in report on an older snapshot will hit the same wall.
- Match 12's `score_data` is now keyed by team id (`{"9008": 2, "9009": 1}`).
  The earlier snapshot had a legacy `{a: 1, b: 1}` row, which our score reader
  renders as `— – —` because it maps team ids first. If any rows like that
  survive elsewhere they will look score-less on the match page.

Every result in section 2 was re-verified after the rollback, so none of it
depends on data that no longer exists.

- The seven screens listed under `BACKEND-GAPS.md` **Delivery required** that
  work in mock mode only — notifications, follows and profile career, leader
  transfer, the admin-user surface, tournament feedback, match comments and
  Pick'em, entry notes. Each says in real mode that the route does not exist
  rather than calling a path that is not there.
- `MatchState` has no member for a thrown-out result. It is slice 1's shared
  vocabulary, so we map `result_rejected` onto `disputed` at our own edge and
  spell out the real state in the panel body. `Disputed` is not quite right —
  the dispute is settled — but it is a great deal closer than `Scheduled`. Worth
  five minutes with slice 1 to decide whether the vocabulary should grow.
